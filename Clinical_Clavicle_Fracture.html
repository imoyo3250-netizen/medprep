Patient Case

A 24-year-old man falls directly onto his right shoulder during a football match. He develops severe pain over the right clavicle, swelling and a palpable irregularity over the middle part of the clavicle. Shoulder movement is painful and limited.

The distal upper limb is warm, with a palpable radial pulse and initially intact motor and sensory function.

🦴 Anatomical Diagnosis

Likely diagnosis: displaced midshaft fracture of the clavicle.

The clavicle functions as a strut connecting the upper limb to the axial skeleton. The middle part is particularly vulnerable because it is relatively less protected by surrounding soft tissue.

Why do the fragments move?

  • Medial fragment: sternocleidomastoid tends to elevate it.
  • Lateral fragment: the weight of the upper limb contributes to inferior displacement.
  • Shoulder mechanics: loss of the clavicular strut can produce shortening and altered shoulder-girdle mechanics.

⚠️ Important structures at risk

  • Subclavian vessels lie deep to the clavicular region.
  • The brachial plexus is closely related to the region and can potentially be affected by significant displacement.
  • Supraclavicular nerves are closely related to the clavicle and may contribute to persistent sensory symptoms.

🩺 Clinical examination

  1. Inspect for swelling, bruising and deformity.
  2. Palpate the clavicle for tenderness and a step-off.
  3. Assess shoulder movement.
  4. Check radial pulse and distal perfusion.
  5. Assess upper-limb motor and sensory function.
  6. Document neurovascular status before and after manipulation or treatment.

🚨 Complications

  • Neurovascular injury involving structures deep to the clavicle.
  • Malunion or non-union.
  • Persistent pain or sensory symptoms.
  • Shortening with altered shoulder mechanics.
  • Skin tenting and, in severe cases, risk of open injury.

🎯 Quick Clinical Questions

1. Which fragment is commonly elevated by sternocleidomastoid?

Medial fragment.

2. Why must neurovascular status be checked?

Because displaced clavicular fragments can threaten nearby subclavian vessels and the brachial plexus.

3. Why can shoulder movement be painful?

The clavicle participates in the shoulder girdle and transmits forces between the upper limb and axial skeleton; movement stresses the injured region.
